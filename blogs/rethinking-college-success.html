Rethinking college success: prestige, purpose, and the skills that actually matter

Louis Newman
College Success Coach

The evidence here challenges several deeply held assumptions about higher education, most notably that institutional prestige and declared major are the primary drivers of college outcomes. They aren't. What emerges instead is a more honest picture: success in college, and beyond, depends far more on institutional fit, self-awareness, interpersonal adaptability, and the deliberate development of transferable thinking skills.

For prospective students and families, the practical implications are real. Evaluating schools using metrics such as faculty accessibility and engagement with students, the availability of high impact educational opportunities (e.g., research, internships, off-campus programs and community engagement courses) and financial support structures yields more meaningful comparisons than rankings alone. Entering college undecided and treating that uncertainty as intellectual honesty rather than a deficiency positions students far better for authentic exploration.

These insights also reflect a broader shift in academic thinking: away from credential-as-outcome and toward learning-as-process. The deeper question worth sitting with is this: if the most valuable things college develops can't be easily measured, what does that tell us about how we've been measuring college success?
